# Story Patterns

The Story layer is the narrative spine. Most companies fit one of five shapes. Pick the shape first, then fill it with specific scenes — concrete time, place, dialogue, decisions. Vague biography is the failure mode.

## The Five Shapes

### 1. Founder Myth — outsider with a thesis

The company exists because the founder saw something others missed and bet their career on it. The origin scene is almost always a moment of rejection, frustration, or outsider insight.

- **Tell**: what did the founder see that others didn't? What were they doing right before the company started? Who laughed at them first?
- **Avoid**: "He had entrepreneurial spirit from a young age." The myth is in the specifics, not the adjectives.
- **Examples**: Jensen Huang (Denny's meeting 1993), 段永平 (subbrand 小霸王 → 步步高 split over ownership), Patrick & John Collison (rejected by 14 VCs before YC).

### 2. Pivot Arc — the thing became something else

The company started as A, hit a wall, found B hiding inside A, and B became the business. The interesting part is the *discovery mechanism* — what feedback revealed the pivot.

- **Tell**: what was the original thesis? What data/customer signal forced the pivot? How did the team resist or embrace the turn?
- **Avoid**: making the pivot sound inevitable. It never is — in real time it's terrifying.
- **Examples**: Slack (Tiny Speck's Glitch game → internal chat tool), Instagram (Burbn check-in app → photo filters), NVIDIA (gaming GPU vendor → CUDA → AI accelerator).

### 3. Counter-Trend — did the opposite and won

Everyone chased one direction; this company chased the other. The story is about conviction under peer pressure.

- **Tell**: what was the "obvious" move? What did this company do instead, and on what evidence? Who on the team pushed the unpopular choice?
- **Avoid**: retrofitting a counter-trend narrative onto a company that just got lucky.
- **Examples**: 拼多多 (下沉市场 while everyone chased 消费升级), Netflix (streaming bet while DVD was still printing money), Costco (kept margins ~11% when peers stretched).

### 4. Wave-Rider — right place, right moment, right tempo

A macro wave (mobile, cloud, LLM, short-video) was coming; this company was positioned to ride it and kept re-accelerating. The story is about *tempo* and reading the wave.

- **Tell**: which wave? When did they spot it vs when did the mainstream? How did they avoid the "too early" trap or the "missed the boat" trap?
- **Avoid**: pure "they were lucky" framing. The interesting question is why *they* caught it and not competitors.
- **Examples**: 字节 (mobile + algorithm recs), Shopify (e-commerce platform wave), Anthropic & OpenAI (transformer → LLM wave).

### 5. Platform Play — built the rails

The company sells picks and shovels to a gold rush. The story is about how they won developer/merchant/creator trust early and compounded network effects.

- **Tell**: who were the first 100 customers, and why did they take the bet? What about the product/terms made switching costly later?
- **Avoid**: treating it as pure tech story. Platforms are social systems — the politics of who hosts what matter.
- **Examples**: Stripe (developer-first APIs), AWS (internal infra productized), Shopify (anti-Amazon merchants), 微信小程序 (超级 App 内嵌入口).

## How to pick

Most companies are dominantly one shape but touched by another. Pick the **dominant** one as the spine; mention the secondary as a subplot.

Check your pick against three questions:

1. If you removed this shape, does the company still make sense? (If yes, wrong shape.)
2. Does the shape surface the *decisions*, not just the timeline?
3. Does it let you end with a judgment that's specific to this company, not a generic "founder vision + execution" conclusion?

## Writing style inside the Story layer

- Prefer scenes over summaries. A 200-word scene beats a 2000-word biography.
- Dialogue is allowed if sourced (interviews, leaked memos, books). Tag `[推测]` if inferred.
- Name at least 3 specific moments: the origin, the pivotal decision, the near-miss or crisis.
- Tie each scene to a mechanism that shows up again in the Logic layer — so the story isn't decoration.
